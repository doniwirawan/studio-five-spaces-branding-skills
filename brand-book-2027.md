## 01 / Foundation & voice

### The identity at a glance

- **Tagline:** create. collab. cowork.
- **Design principle:** Blackout sets the scene. Electric Purple leads. White gives information room to breathe. Photography shows the creative experience.
- **Visual system:** Sora + Inter.
- **Voice:** Trendy. Youthful. Electric energy. Authentic. Experiential. Precise about the experience we offer.

### Why the identity evolved

| Principle | Meaning |
|---|---|
| A distinct entity | The original SPACES wordmark and Electric Purple identify the entity. Repeat them consistently across every touchpoint. |
| A creative experience | The brand revolves around *create. collab. cowork.* Show studio work, collaboration and the creator routine. |
| A clear hierarchy | Blackout leads the experience. Electric carries recognition. Glow, Deep UV, Matte Black and White support the content. |
| One shared standard | Use the same artwork, Sora and Inter, spacing and voice across digital, print and the physical studio. |

### What Studio Five Spaces does

- **CREATE.** Photo and video studios support photography, filming, podcasts and other content creation.
- **COLLAB.** Creators, collaborators and teams use the studios to make work together. Show the people and the output.
- **COWORK.** Coworking gives creators a working base alongside the studio. Describe the actual plan and facilities.

### Naming & website endorsement

- **Use the complete name.** Write *Studio Five Spaces* in full in formal copy. Use the supplied SPACES artwork for visual identity.
- **Use the exact tagline.** `create. collab. cowork.` — keep the wording, sequence and full stops. The footer uses the clean logo alone.
- **Website footer / shared structure.** Left: entity logo. Middle: navigation and contact links. Right: *part of* with the Group logo, linked to studiofive.group.
- **Balanced endorsement.** Keep *part of* left-aligned with the Group artwork. Use Inter Regular 13 px / 1.2 line height with a 6 px gap before the logo.
- **Desktop to mobile.** Desktop logos: SPACES 160 px / Group 136 px. Mobile: stack entity, links, endorsement, legal. Side padding: 24 px.

Approved desktop footer (three zones):

| Explore | Get in touch | part of |
|---|---|---|
| Our studios — Photo and video studios. | Book a studio | Studio Five Group logo |
| Coworking — Coworking in Singapore. | WhatsApp | |
| Contact — Email the SPACES team | Email us | |

Legal line: `© 2026 Studio Five Spaces Pte Ltd. All rights reserved. · studiofivespaces.com · Terms & conditions`

### Our five voice traits

| Trait | Do | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Trendy | Use current, relevant creative references. | Forced slang or distracting jargon. |
| Youthful | Use direct, active and inviting language. | Childish phrasing or talking down. |
| Electric energy | Use purposeful verbs and a confident pace. | Constant capitals and exclamation marks. |
| Authentic | State specific, supportable details. | Hype, invented scarcity or vague claims. |
| Experiential | Describe what people can do and make. | Abstract promises without an experience. |

Use these traits together. Context changes the intensity; accuracy, clarity and the original identity remain consistent.

### Make the next action clear

| Context | Avoid | Use | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Booking invitation | Unlock an unparalleled world of limitless creativity. | Explore the studios. Choose the setup for your next shoot. | Names the offer and a useful next step. |
| Studio tour caption | The most iconic creative destination ever. | Take a look inside the studio before planning your shoot. | Sets a clear expectation without an unsupported claim. |
| Booking action | Discover more about our amazing possibilities. | Explore the studios | Identifies the destination clearly. |

Functional headlines may vary by purpose. **create. collab. cowork.** is the only recurring tagline; never introduce an alternative slogan.

## 02 / Logo & slogan

### The Spaces primary wordmark

- **Primary / horizontal wordmark:** Use the original outlined SPACES artwork. Keep its wide silhouette, paths and letter spacing. Never rebuild the lettering with a font.
- **Standalone S icon:** Use the supplied standalone S master in compact contexts where the company is already named. Keep the S free of an added square backing.

### Six approved logo versions

| # | Version |
|---|---|
| 01 | Wordmark / Electric |
| 02 | Wordmark / White |
| 03 | S standalone / Electric |
| 04 | S standalone / White |
| 05 | S circular / Electric |
| 06 | S circular / White |

Use the supplied master for each version. Circular versions have fixed White / Electric pairings; never rebuild their circle or S scale.

### The right logo for the background

| Background | Logo |
|---|---|
| **Blackout (first choice)** | Electric is preferred. White monochrome is also allowed. Preserve the original paths. |
| **White / light** | Use Electric, Deep UV or Matte Black. |
| **Electric Purple** | Use the complete wordmark in White monochrome. |

### Horizontal wordmark in a banner

- **Use the wordmark master.** Place the current wordmark in a wide, padded field. A banner is a layout, not a separate logo.
- **Keep icon artwork separate.** Use the supplied circular S for profiles or badges. Never add a square to the standalone S.

### Clear space, minimum sizes & icons

- **Clear space:** X = one quarter of visible wordmark height. Keep at least X around the mark; prefer 2X for larger uses.
- **Minimum sizes:**
  - Wordmark: 120 px / 25 mm wide
  - Banner: 240 px / 50 mm wide
  - S icon: 24 px / 6 mm high
- **Icon containers** (standalone S, Electric circle, White circle): Use the standalone S by default. Use a supplied circular S for a profile or functional badge; never redraw its proportions. Use each supplied master intact: letterform, internal space and circular colour pairing. Never add a square.

### One tagline. Three arrangements.

| Arrangement | Rule |
|---|---|
| 01 / Default — **Logo alone** | The original SPACES logo is complete without the slogan. This is the default. |
| 02 / Supporting slogan — **Logo leads** | Inter Medium, centred beneath the logo at 73% of its width. Optional and separate. |
| 03 / Campaign copy — **Copy leads** | Large Sora copy leads. Keep the logo separate; do not repeat the slogan. |

Exact wording: **create. collab. cowork.** Keep lowercase, order and full stops. The slogan never becomes part of the core logo.

### Use the slogan when it adds meaning

| Application | Default | When to include it |
|---|---|---|
| Website hero | Optional | Introduce the overall SPACES experience. |
| Deck cover / closing | Optional | Introduce or sign off a brand story. |
| Content slide / thumbnail | Omit | Let the subject or useful information lead. |
| Poster / campaign | Optional | Support the logo or become the main copy. |
| Video intro / outro | Optional | Choose a deliberate moment; favour one use. |
| Documents / signatures | Omit | Keep functional information clear and compact. |

Use once per layout. One line wherever readable; stack only in genuinely narrow formats. A large headline never needs a second tagline beneath the logo.

### Keep the supporting slogan subordinate

- **Example master:** 600-unit wordmark; slogan 438 units wide in Inter Medium, about 40 units in size. Centre to the visible wordmark; normal tracking.
- **Readable at final size:** Minimum slogan 16 CSS px / 9 pt. At this ratio, allow at least 240 px / 48 mm wordmark width. Otherwise omit the slogan.
- **One supporting arrangement:** Original vector logo + separate Inter text. Always centre underneath — never left-align or stretch to full logo width. Gap: wordmark width / 18. Keep clear space; use Medium, not Bold.

Guides explain logo clear space and are not artwork. Use Sora for a large campaign statement; these supporting settings do not limit headline scale.

### Logo misuse / shape & typography

- ✗ Do not stretch
- ✗ Do not rotate
- ✗ Do not crop
- ✗ Do not rearrange
- ✗ Do not retype
- ✗ Do not add a square

Use original paths and proportions. The seasonal allowance never permits retyping, replacing or obscuring letters.

### Photography, shadows & logo effects

- **Choose the background first.** Find a quieter crop and select the clearest colourway. A White wordmark is often the strongest photo overlay. Prefer a quiet area or solid reading field.
- **A restrained exception.** A subtle Blackout shadow may support separation on a photo or video. Keep it secondary and test the final crop.
- **Limits.** Keep all letterforms clear. No coloured glow, hard outline, bevel or multiple shadows. Reposition if a shadow cannot resolve visibility — a shadow cannot fix a light, busy crop; reposition or change colourway.

### Seasonal logo expressions

- **Occasional and purposeful.** Use a seasonal version for a specific campaign or occasion. It is optional and has a defined end date.
- **One considered addition.** Keep every original letter visible. Use one accessory outside the outlines; measure clear space around the complete composition. Use only the existing palette.
- **Controlled applications.** Use on temporary web or social campaigns. Keep permanent signage, signatures and business documents on the standard identity. Keep the original master file intact and store seasonal additions separately.

## 03 / Colour system

### Body text follows the background

| Background | Body text | Supporting use |
|---|---|---|
| **Blackout** | White — for paragraphs and detailed reading. | Glow for selected links, labels and emphasis. |
| **White** | Matte Black — for paragraphs and detailed reading. | Deep UV for links and short functional text. |
| **Electric** | White — for readable information on an Electric field. | Keep the message focused and the hierarchy simple. |

**Electric text on Blackout or White: large text only.**

### The six-colour palette

| Colour | HEX | RGB | Role | Use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Electric Purple | `#A21FFF` | 162 / 31 / 255 | Primary | Entity recognition |
| Glow | `#C9A8FF` | 201 / 168 / 255 | Secondary | Light support |
| Deep UV | `#4C1D95` | 76 / 29 / 149 | Secondary | Purple links |
| Blackout | `#17141A` | 23 / 20 / 26 | Neutral | Lead surface |
| Matte Black | `#231F20` | 35 / 31 / 32 | Neutral | Body on White |
| White | `#FFFFFF` | 255 / 255 / 255 | Neutral | Light surface |

Exact sRGB values for digital use. Physical colour follows the agreed print process and proof. *White* is the colour name throughout this book.

```css
:root {
  --electric-purple: #A21FFF;
  --glow:            #C9A8FF;
  --deep-uv:         #4C1D95;
  --blackout:        #17141A;
  --matte-black:     #231F20;
  --white:           #FFFFFF;
}
```

### The colour hierarchy

*Blackout sets the scene. Electric Purple leads.*

- **Default mode — Blackout-first.** Start websites and decks with Blackout. Use the Electric logo and large headlines, with White body text.
- **Supporting modes — White / information and Electric / campaign.** White serves detailed reading. Electric creates selected campaign moments. Glow and Deep UV support the message.

No universal percentage is mandatory. Select the mode for the content, then assess the visible flat-colour balance.

### Colour behaviour by context

| Context | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| Website | Blackout leads the site. The Electric logo is the preferred identity. White and Electric sections are allowed when content calls for them. |
| Presentations | Open with Blackout. Use Spaces branding throughout. Change to White for detailed reading or Electric for a focused campaign moment. |
| Posters & social | Use Blackout, White or Electric to support the image and message. Electric remains the main expressive purple; avoid equal colour competition. |
| Supporting colours | Use Glow for readable purple detail on Blackout. Use Deep UV for links and short functional text on White. Matte Black carries long copy on White. |

Background choice does not change the six-colour palette, logo proportions or type system. Ratios are assessed by application, not imposed on every page.

### Readable colour combinations

**01 / Electric on Blackout** — the first-choice expressive pair. Use Electric for large text and the logo. White carries body copy; Glow supports readable purple detail.

| Pair | Ratio |
|---|---|
| Electric on Blackout / large | 3.63:1 |
| White on Blackout / body | 18.24:1 |
| Glow on Blackout / detail | 9.14:1 |

**02 / Electric on White** — reserve Electric for large text and the original logo. Small text uses Matte Black or Deep UV under the brand rules, even though Electric passes AA.

| Pair | Ratio |
|---|---|
| Electric on White / large | 5.03:1 |
| Matte Black on White / body | 16.30:1 |
| Deep UV on White / links | 10.95:1 |

**03 / Small-text defaults** — White on Blackout or Electric (White on Electric / body: 5.03:1). Matte Black paragraphs on White; Deep UV links and short text on White.

Large text: at least 24 CSS px regular or 18.67 CSS px bold at final display size. Brand permissions may be stricter than a passing contrast result.

### See the colour roles in context

1. **Original logo** — keep the original artwork intact.
2. **Large headline** — use Electric for a large headline (e.g. "Photo & video studios").
3. **Body copy** — White paragraphs; comfortable line lengths (e.g. "Explore the studios, compare setups and plan your session.").
4. **Action button** — White label on an Electric button (e.g. "Explore the studios").

### Digital colour & print production

| Topic | Rule |
|---|---|
| Digital reference | Use the exact HEX and RGB values in sRGB. Electric Purple is the screen hero. Never sample a screenshot or preview. |
| Process printing | Deep UV is the supplied process-print alternative. **C82 M95 Y0 K0** is a starting build to proof, not a universal match for screen Electric. |
| Spot-colour candidates | **Pantone 2592 C** and **Pantone Violet C** are candidates. Confirm ink, stock and finish on a physical proof before specifying a match. |
| Fabric & finishing | Purple ink on dark fabric may need a white underbase. Test the actual print or embroidery; use original monochrome vectors for finishing. |

Confirm supplier, profile, material, finish and proof. Screen Electric cannot be promised as an identical four-colour process result.

### Assign every colour a job

| Colour | Job |
|---|---|
| Electric Purple | Recognition: original logo, large headlines, main actions and selected campaign fields. |
| Blackout | The lead surface for websites and decks. Use White paragraphs and an Electric identity. |
| Glow | Readable purple detail on Blackout: links, labels and selective emphasis. |
| Matte Black | Paragraphs, booking instructions, terms and detailed tables on White. |
| Deep UV | Links, short functional text and purple detail on White; supplied print starting alternative. |
| White | A reading surface and negative space. White text works on Blackout and Electric. |

Use White labels on Electric buttons. Neutrals support the three purples; no fixed split between neutral colours is mandatory.

### Make information visible

- **Give colour a function.** Use colour for hierarchy, navigation or data. No decorative motif, recurring bar pattern or symbol-derived texture is part of Spaces.
- **Protect essential content.** Text, icons, controls and chart values need sufficient contrast. Give light fills a visible boundary when shape carries meaning (e.g. Deep UV boundary on White).
- **Use more than colour.** Pair meaning with labels, values or icons. Underline links. A viewer must understand an error or status without distinguishing purple shades. Actions: White label on Electric, with a contrasting focus ring.

WCAG 1.4.1 / 1.4.11: meaning and interaction must remain visible. Functional table rules and grid guides are not decorative motifs.

### Contrast limits for every palette pair

| Pair (both directions) | Ratio | WCAG result / brand limit |
|---|---|---|
| Electric / Blackout | 3.63:1 | Electric text: large only / logo permitted |
| Electric / Glow | 2.52:1 | No text or essential graphics |
| Electric / Deep UV | 2.18:1 | No text or essential graphics |
| Electric / Matte Black | 3.24:1 | Electric text: large only / logo permitted |
| Electric / White | 5.03:1 | Electric: large only; White on Electric: normal |
| Glow / Deep UV | 5.49:1 | Normal text and essential graphics |
| Glow / Blackout | 9.14:1 | Normal text and essential graphics |
| Glow / Matte Black | 8.17:1 | Normal text and essential graphics |
| Glow / White | 1.99:1 | No text or essential graphics |
| Deep UV / Blackout | 1.66:1 | No text or essential graphics |
| Deep UV / Matte Black | 1.49:1 | No text or essential graphics |
| Deep UV / White | 10.95:1 | Normal text and essential graphics |
| Blackout / Matte Black | 1.12:1 | No text or essential graphics |
| Blackout / White | 18.24:1 | Normal text and essential graphics |
| Matte Black / White | 16.30:1 | Normal text and essential graphics |

Calculated from exact sRGB. Normal text needs 4.5:1; large text / essential graphics 3:1. Electric text is restricted to large sizes by Spaces policy. White/Electric meets normal-text contrast in either direction; only the White-on-Electric direction is permitted for small text.

### Colour do's & don'ts

| Do / follow the roles | Avoid / breaking the roles |
|---|---|
| **Blackout first** — Electric for large type and identity. | **Small Electric text** — no Electric body text on Blackout or White. |
| **White body text** — White paragraphs on Blackout. | **Glow on White** — do not use Glow as reading text on White. |
| **Matte Black + Deep UV** — Matte Black paragraphs; Deep UV links on White. | **Equal colour competition** — give each colour a specific purpose. |
| **Clear actions** — White label on an Electric button. | **Colour-only status** — explain a state with words or icons. |
| **Exact values** — use the six approved digital tokens. | **Improvised colours** — do not add extra tints or accent tokens. |
| **Actual pairing** — check the final field and image crop. | **Unchecked overlay** — a shadow cannot fix every photograph. |

A technically passing contrast result is not automatically a permitted body-text treatment. Colour roles and readability both apply.

### Measure the designed surface area

- **Count visible flat fields.** Include background and graphic fields. Exclude photography; include solid overlays. Count only the visible portion of overlaps.
- **Ignore overlaid letters.** Measure the field beneath logos, text and icons. Divide each colour area by the total flat-field area.
- **Assess the composition.** Start with the chosen mode and review the balance.

*Illustration only:* 7,000 Blackout / 2,000 Electric / 1,000 White out of 10,000 visible flat-field units = 70% / 20% / 10%. This explains the method; it is not a universal colour quota. Glow, Deep UV and Matte Black are available when their supporting roles are needed.

## 04 / Typography

### Two typefaces. Clear jobs.

| Role | Typeface | Use | Tracking |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 / Headlines | **Sora** | Headlines and expressive campaign copy. | Large: −2%. Smaller: −1%. Inspect letter pairs. |
| 02 / Information | **Inter** | Paragraphs, instructions and practical details. | 0 tracking. Never stretch text to fill a line. |
| 03 / Labels & actions | **Inter Medium** | Navigation, buttons and short functional labels. | Uppercase labels: +6%. Keep the label short and distinct. |

Use real font weights; keep the logo as outlines.

### Sora & Inter

- **Sora (titles & headings):** Medium, SemiBold and Bold. Use the supplied files and real weights.
- **Inter (body & information):** Regular, Medium, SemiBold and Bold.
- **Logo:** original outlined artwork.

### Sizes, weights & line heights

| Format | Display / H1 / H2 | Body / caption |
|---|---|---|
| Website desktop | 64–80 / 48–56 / 32–40 px | 18 px / 14 px |
| Website mobile | 40–48 / 32–36 / 24–28 px | 16–18 px / 13–14 px |
| Deck: 1440 × 810 | 96–112 / 54–60 / 32–36 units | 24–28 / 18–20 units |
| A4 documents | 36–44 / 24–28 / 16–20 pt | 10.5–12 pt / 8.5–9.5 pt |

- **Weight:** Display and H1: 700. H2: 600. Minor headings: 500–600. Body: 400. Inter labels: 500–700.
- **Line height:** Sora: 100–110%. Inter body: 140–150%. Captions: 130–140%.

### See the hierarchy at work (desktop website specimen)

| Level | Spec | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Headline / Sora Bold | 56 px size / 56 px leading / −2% tracking. Establishes the first reading level. | Photo & video studios |
| Body / Inter Regular | 18 px size / 27 px leading / 0 tracking. | Explore the studios, compare setups and plan your session. |
| Action / Inter Medium | 18 px. Glow on Blackout; underline an inline text link. | Explore the studios |

Other starting points: Mobile — Inter body 16–18 px / caption 13–14 px. 1440 × 810 deck — Inter body 24–28 / caption 18–20 units. A specification is checked at the actual output size.

### Tracking, kerning & spacing

- **Headlines:** Large Sora headings −0.02em; smaller headings −0.01em. Use metrics or optical kerning, then inspect awkward pairs.
- **Body & labels:** Inter body 0em. Uppercase labels +0.06em. Never stretch letters or track paragraphs to force a fit.
- **Summary:** Title −2% | Body 0% | Labels +6%. *Sora for expression. Inter for information.*
- **Spacing rhythm:** Use 8, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64 and 96 units. Keep related elements close and leave a larger gap between groups. Check exported spacing, line breaks and punctuation at the final viewing size.

### Editorial & font handling

| Topic | Rule |
|---|---|
| Typesetting | Left-align paragraphs. Start with 45–75 characters per line. Keep headings concise and avoid widows, isolated words and justified text. |
| Emphasis | Matte Black carries paragraphs on White. Deep UV carries links and short functional text. Electric text is large only. Underline inline links. |
| Font availability | Use Sora and Inter with real weights. Embed the fonts in PDFs and inspect the exported appearance before release. |
| Tagline & artwork | Use *create. collab. cowork.* on one line where it fits. Stack only in narrow formats. Keep the wordmark as original outlines. |

If a required language lacks characters in the supplied fonts, use a suitable companion and review it with a fluent reader.

## 05 / Layout, imagery & video

### Grid, margins & safe zones

- **Presentation master:** 1440 × 810 units. Side margins: 64. Footer boundary: y752. Minimum main gutter: 48. Use an 8-unit rhythm.
- **Web grid:** Desktop — 12 columns, 24–32 px gutters and 64–80 px side padding. Mobile — 4 columns and 16–24 px side padding.
- **Other formats:** Start with a 5% safe zone. Check bleed and platform overlays separately. Logo clear space still applies.

Grid guides are not artwork.

### Composition & hierarchy

- **Give each group space.** Keep label and body together. Leave at least 40 units after independent groups; give tables equal row padding.
- **Choose the composition.** Use a hero statement, split image/text layout or columns according to the message. Keep one dominant focal point.
- **Resolve wrapping.** Edit or redistribute copy when it wraps. Preserve the same group rhythm; never let a paragraph crowd the next heading.

### Keep headings with their explanations

- **Avoid:** topics run together — the next heading is too close to the preceding explanation (small gap between different topics).
- **Use:** related items stay together — longer copy pushes the next group down; repeat this relationship.

Protect the gaps. Reflow or split the page when content grows. Example assignment: **24 within / 48 between**, using the existing spacing scale. Do not impose those numbers on every format; preserve the relationship.

### Choose the layout for the message

| Layout | Use when |
|---|---|
| Image-led | The studio or creative work carries the main message. |
| Information-led | Details, specifications and booking instructions. |
| Statement-led | A deliberate brand moment. The tagline functions as copy here. |

All three layouts use the same underlying grid. Composition can vary; typography, logo integrity, colour roles and clear spacing remain stable.

### Use the actual studio as the reference

- **In this brand book:** Use genuine studio photography and clearly labelled concepts to illustrate the identity.
- **Choose with purpose:** Show the room, equipment, light and usable space. Preserve spatial context and material colour.
- **Retouch with restraint:** Clean incidental damage or distractions. Preserve the real studio; do not invent facilities or flood it with purple.

### Keep creative work in its own context

- **Presentation decks:** Creator collaborations may demonstrate the work made in the space. Select images for the particular story and composition.
- **When colour competes:** If a dominant orange or other treatment fights a page, choose another image. Consistent framing helps organisation; it does not remove that colour.
- **Respect the source:** Preserve intentional creative treatment and creator credits. Studio imagery defines the reference; creator imagery illustrates a specific story.

### Photography, type & functional icons

- **No decorative motif.** Do not add recurring bars, patterns, logo-derived textures or ornamental line systems. Build identity with the logo, type, colour and image.
- **Functional icons.** Use one outline family. Start at 24 px with 1.5–2 px strokes. Pair unfamiliar icons with a label and visible contrast.
- **Purposeful shapes only.** Use the supplied circular S for a profile crop or useful document badge. A seasonal accessory is a separate, temporary allowance.
- **Visual ingredients:** Original logo. Sora. Inter. Six colours. Real studio imagery.

### Video — make the studio experience tangible

- **Show the experience.** Open on a strong result. Reveal the setup, the creators and the detail that makes the session work.
- **Use image and sound.** Capture light changes, hands adjusting equipment and studio sound. Edit with intention and give each detail time to register.
- **Close consistently.** Use Electric on Blackout or White on Electric. Optional slogan: Inter Medium, centred below at 73% width. Keep captions readable. (Example end frame: 16:9.)

### Choose one clear brand moment

- **Logo alone / default:** A clean sign-off works without a slogan. Keep the original artwork and leave enough time for recognition.
- **Optional supporting slogan:** Centre Inter Medium below the wordmark at 73% width. Keep it separate; omit a repeated intro treatment.

Caption essential speech and keep text clear of platform overlays. Preview the motion and final export; no fixed universal outro duration is required.

## 06 / Website, presentations & other applications

### A Blackout-first website

- **Lead with Blackout.** Use the Electric logo and large headline on Blackout. White carries reading text.
- **Show the actual studios.** Use genuine facility images, clear booking details and specific actions (navigation example: Studio · Collabs · Cowork · Book; CTA: "Book a studio").
- **Footer endorsement.** Use the approved three-zone footer: small entity logo left, links middle, *part of* + Group logo right. Stack in that order on mobile.

### Responsive design & accessibility

| Topic | Rule |
|---|---|
| Reading | Use White on Blackout. On White, use Matte Black paragraphs and Deep UV links or short text. Mobile body copy starts at 16–18 px. |
| Actions & focus | Use specific action labels and White text on Electric buttons. On Blackout, use a Glow focus ring with a dark gap; on White, Deep UV with a White gap. |
| Interaction | Underline links. Pair status with words or icons. Essential controls need a visible boundary; Glow on White is insufficient. |
| Responsive identity | Keep the original wordmark clear in wide headers. Use an unframed S where the company name is visible. Preview mobile crops and touch targets. |

Check keyboard access, accessible names, image descriptions, reduced motion and the final foreground/background pair.

### Article covers — purple lives in the scene

Default: photograph above, heading and summary below. Brand the scene with a subtle but noticeable purple detail.

1. **Default article card.** Place the photograph above the heading and summary. Upload a clean image; keep the words as editable website text.
2. **Purple within the scene.** Before upload, recolour a selected object if needed. Keep the purple noticeable, with natural material, light and texture.
3. **Creative flexibility.** Other web layouts may overlay live text when readable. Keep it separate from the image file; a baked-in thumbnail graphic is not required.

Example cards: *What is a creator hub?* — A workspace and a content-production studio under one roof. · *Podcast & content studio* — A space to record and film your content. · *A 24-hour creative studio* — Create, collaborate and work in the same building.

### Spaces branding in every deck

- **Direct proposals:** Use the Spaces identity. Blackout first. Original Electric wordmark. Sora headings and Inter detail. The exact tagline is optional. Use White or Electric pages when useful.
- **Tenders & client presentations:** Keep Spaces branding. Do not adopt the client colour palette, fonts or visual identity. Identify the client through project text and required factual references.

### Keep the deck grid fixed

- **Page and chrome:** 1440 × 810 units. Side margins: 64. Keep labels, rules, footers and page numbers in the same positions.
- **Content positions:** Title at x64 / y117. Begin content at y224 or below and end by y712. Keep the footer boundary at y752 clear.
- **Columns and group spacing:** Two columns of 632 units each, with a 48-unit gutter. Keep at least 40 units between successive text groups.

Sora H1: 54–60 units. Inter body: 24–28. Use equal row heights and consistent insets for repeated information.

### Write one clear point per slide

- **Use a useful headline.** State the takeaway. Keep titles concise; a second line must move content down rather than collide with it.
- **Make detail readable.** Start with 3–5 short points. Keep body type at 24 units or above; move background detail to notes or an appendix.
- **Resolve overflow.** Edit, split the slide or change the layout. Preserve type size and group spacing rather than packing the footer.
- **Avoid:** unrelated detail with equal emphasis — one slide should not carry a studio overview, pricing table, equipment list, full terms and campaign message at the same level.

If one repeated row wraps, revise the group consistently. Check the space after paragraphs as well as the space before them.

### Make evidence easy to assess

- **Choose relevant work.** Use genuine studio and creator images. Preserve key details and credits. Show a different view when it adds evidence.
- **State what data means.** Name the finding, unit, period and source. Distinguish measured results, estimates, targets and examples.
- **Protect essential graphics.** Use Deep UV or Matte Black on White for detail. Add visible boundaries to Glow marks and label every meaningful series.

Required evidence fields: finding / unit / date or period / source · status: measured, estimate, target or example · permission: image, creator and testimonial.

Verify claims and permissions before release. A concept visual does not establish a current facility, result or service commitment.

- **Scope and responsibilities:** Name deliverables, quantity, format, owner and deadline. State inputs, revision limits, exclusions and approvals.
- **Schedule and dependencies:** Use confirmed dates and stages. Make dependencies clear; avoid presenting assumed resources as commitments.
- **Commercial clarity:** State currency, fees, inclusions, exclusions, tax treatment and validity. Confirm the details before release.

#### Reels — let the words play

## 09 / Brand review checklist

### Check the brand. Then check the design.

A practical review for designers, marketing and anyone publishing SPACES work.

1. **Every piece** — check the core identity: logo, colour, type, slogan, copy, imagery and layout.
2. **Your format** — check the application: extra checks for web, decks, social, video or print.
3. **Final export** — review the actual result: confirm compliance, then judge clarity, balance and impact.

Use all core checks + the checks for your format. Do not tick an item you have not verified.

### Every piece starts here — the eight essentials

Use N/A only when an element is absent.

- [ ] **Logo** — Supplied artwork, correct proportions and colourway. Required clear space; no unapproved effects.
- [ ] **Purpose + voice** — One clear message. Trendy, youthful, electric energy, authentic and experiential; specific, truthful copy.
- [ ] **Colour + contrast** — Exact palette values. Check the pairing for the text size; Electric is not a small body-text colour.
- [ ] **Imagery** — Image supports the story. Preserve real facilities and creator treatment; distinguish concepts from reality.
- [ ] **Typography** — Sora and Inter. Clear heading, body and supporting levels; comfortable line spacing and readable size.
- [ ] **Spacing + alignment** — Align repeated elements. Keep headings near their own text; leave larger gaps between unrelated groups.
- [ ] **Slogan** — Exact wording: *create. collab. cowork.* Optional unless the specific application requires it.
- [ ] **Hierarchy + restraint** — One dominant focal point. Avoid competing effects or decorative motifs. Give text and images room.

Reading text: White on Blackout; Matte Black or Deep UV on White. Check the approved colour chart for other pairings.

### Keep the identity. Adapt the expression.

| Element | Keep fixed | Designers may adapt |
|---|---|---|
| Logo | Master geometry, proportions, clear space. | Approved variant, size and placement. |
| Colour | Palette values and permitted pairings. | Background and emphasis for the content. |
| Type + copy | Sora / Inter; voice; exact slogan wording. | Headline, scale and hierarchy within the rules. |
| Layout | Grid discipline, safe zones, group spacing. | Composition, image scale and content flow. |
| Photography | Truthful context and useful image quality. | Subject, crop and restrained retouching. |
| Effects | Logo protections; no decorative motif. | Permitted headline effects with a clear purpose. |

A different composition can be compliant. An attractive design can still break a rule. Review both separately.

### Check the format you are making

- [ ] **Website** — Blackout-first direction; readable body text and clear actions. Check desktop and mobile. Approved footer: entity left, links middle, Group endorsement right.
- [ ] **Presentation** — Start with Blackout. Keep SPACES branding throughout. Check heading-to-body gaps, content density, alignment and slide-to-slide rhythm.
- [ ] **Social + thumbnails** — One subject and clear hook. Preview crops and interface overlays. Follow the standard or trend-led route; use Apple iOS-family emoji where required.
- [ ] **Video** — Check captions, reading time and interface overlays. Keep a clear brand moment. Use the approved logo or supporting slogan arrangement when appropriate.
- [ ] **Print + merchandise** — Use the approved print translation, not screen Electric as a direct CMYK target. Check final dimensions, bleed, safe margins and artwork quality.
- [ ] **Photography + concepts** — Show the real studio accurately. Use images that fit the story. Keep generated or branded concepts clearly distinguished from actual facilities.

For mixed outputs, use more than one group. A social video needs both the social and video checks.

### Review the export. Make a decision.

The maker checks the rules. The reviewer checks whether the work communicates effectively.

| 01 / Compliance | 02 / Design quality |
|---|---|
| **Core checks complete** — confirm each relevant rule against the approved book or master asset. | **Message understood quickly** — can someone identify the main point without an explanation? |
| **Format checks complete** — check the required branding, crop, footer or production treatment. | **Hierarchy + balance work** — check emphasis, reading order, alignment, gaps and optical balance. |
| **Export verified** — open the final file; check missing assets, clipping, wrapping and image quality. | **Works at the real viewing size** — review on a phone, screen or print proof as appropriate to the output. |

**Decision:** **READY** — relevant checks passed. · **REVISE** — rule broken or message unclear. · **CLARIFY** — no approved rule fits.

Unresolved exceptions go to the brand owner. Do not silently turn a one-off treatment into a new rule.
